Write a rigorous, self-contained reference solution to the mathematical
problem below, represented as an adaptive parallel reasoning trace.

The trace should demonstrate how to solve the problem through a useful
combination of sequential reasoning, parallel work, synchronization,
and further reasoning.

Prioritize mathematical correctness, completeness, and meaningful progress.

EXECUTION MODEL

The trace represents one coordinator and multiple parallel branches.

The coordinator may:
- reason sequentially;
- launch 2–8 independent branches;
- synchronize their results;
- continue reasoning and launch another round;
- finish when the solution is complete.

Choose the number of branches separately for each round.
Eight is the maximum, not a target.

For a difficult problem, use multiple rounds when earlier results enable
new useful parallel work. Do not force a fixed number of rounds.
Use sequential reasoning when parallelization would merely duplicate work.

Do not choose the total number of rounds in advance.
After each synchronization, decide what work is still necessary.
Continue sequentially or launch another parallel round as appropriate.
Stop when the solution is complete; never add rounds merely to increase depth.
The limit of 8 applies only to simultaneous branches within one round.

PLANNING BEFORE EACH FORK

Before every <Parallel> block, write a short plan containing:
- the round ID: r1, r2, ...;
- the current objective;
- the number of branches;
- a unique ID for every branch: r1.p1, r1.p2, ...;
- each branch's concrete assignment and expected output;
- how the outputs will be combined or evaluated.

The plan must reflect information available before that round.
Do not put the answers to the assigned subproblems into the plan.
Shared definitions and useful common preprocessing are allowed.

Assignments must be specific. Avoid vague instructions such as
“try another approach” without identifying the approach or question.

USEFUL BRANCH ASSIGNMENTS

A branch may:
- solve an independent subproblem needed by the final solution;
- analyze a specified case or region of the domain;
- derive a lemma, bound, invariant, or necessary condition;
- investigate a substantively different promising method;
- test a conjecture or construct a counterexample;
- verify a candidate or claim established before the current round.

Different cases may use the same method.
Rewording the same calculation, changing variable names, or changing
arithmetic order does not constitute useful additional work.

Exploit symmetry and common structure when they eliminate redundant work.

INFORMATION BOUNDARIES

Each branch may use only:
- the original problem;
- the shared context established before the current fork;
- its own assignment and calculations.

Branches in the same round must not use, quote, anticipate, or claim
agreement with one another's results.

If a task requires another branch's result, perform it after synchronization
or assign it in a later round.

This is a serialized representation of parallel execution:
although branch texts appear consecutively, later-listed branches must
remain independent of earlier-listed branches in the same round.

BRANCH CONTENT

Start each branch with its assigned ID.

Provide concise, explicit mathematical work sufficient to verify its result.
Preserve relevant assumptions, units, domain restrictions, and case boundaries.

Complete the assigned task rather than merely describing how it could be done.
Return the requested local result; a branch need not solve the whole problem.

A justified empty case, counterexample, or unsuccessful approach can be useful.
Explain precisely what was established and what remains unresolved.
Do not invent mistakes, dead ends, or disagreements to make the trace longer.

An unproved conjecture must remain labelled as a conjecture.
Do not present numerical evidence as a general proof.
Do not claim to have run tools or computations that were not actually run.

Branches cannot launch nested parallel blocks.

SYNCHRONIZATION

After every </Parallel>, write a <Summary> block.

Synchronization must advance the solution:
- assess branch results and their assumptions;
- refer to contributions using branch IDs;
- combine complementary results;
- check coverage and overlaps when merging cases;
- address contradictions through explicit mathematical reasoning;
- distinguish established facts, hypotheses, and unresolved obligations;
- identify the next useful step.

Do not claim that all branches agree if they produced different kinds of results.
Agreement or majority vote alone is not proof.
Do not attribute a derivation or check to a branch that did not perform it.

Perform any additional connecting calculations explicitly.

A later round should address work made possible or necessary by the evolving
solution: a remaining case, a new subproblem, a surviving conjecture,
a candidate requiring verification, or a gap in the proof.

Do not repeat completed work merely to create another round.

COMPLETION AND QUALITY

Produce a complete solution whenever you can justify one.
Do not fabricate a conclusion if an essential gap remains.

For equations, check admissibility and extraneous solutions.
For exhaustive searches or classifications, justify completeness.
For counting, prevent omissions and double counting.
For optimization, justify optimality rather than only giving a candidate.
For proofs, discharge the necessary assumptions and intermediate claims.

Keep the trace focused:
- no theatrical internal monologue;
- no generic filler about exploring different perspectives;
- no artificial proliferation of branches;
- no retrospective rewriting of earlier plans;
- no unsupported leaps concealed by a correct final answer.
