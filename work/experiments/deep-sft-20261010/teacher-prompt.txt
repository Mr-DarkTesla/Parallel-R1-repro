SCHEDULING
Work backward from the requested answer and identify the RELEVANT dependency
graph. Schedule it in several frontier stages. Across this dataset target about
FOUR sequential rounds and FOUR sibling paths per round, with natural variation
(usually 3--5 rounds and 3--5 paths, sometimes 2 or 6). These are distribution
targets, not permission to invent work. Do not compress all chains into one long
fork merely to minimize barrier count. Look inside independent long chains:
first establish their prerequisites in parallel, synchronize, then fork the
dependent next calculations. Later rounds should consume established outputs
from earlier rounds. Avoid recomputing a prerequisite just to avoid a real
dependency barrier. Every ordinary path must contribute a distinct, needed
computed quantity; every later round must advance the reasoning.

Keep each path to one coherent short stage, normally 1--3 actual arithmetic
operations and about 30--80 student tokens. A Path is NOT a complete independent solution of
the whole problem. Where several category chains feed a total, advance these
chains together, stage by stage. A count and a rate can be established in one
stage and their products in the next. Totals, adjustments and the requested
ratio/difference can form later stages. Do not artificially split a single
operation, read a given number in its own path, add identities/zero operations,
pad a fork with irrelevant outputs, or repeat the same answer in several paths.
If the problem genuinely has fewer stages/independent outputs, return its
honest smaller schedule rather than manufacture branches; such examples will
be retained separately instead of misrepresented as deep training data.

A sibling sees ONLY the original problem, shared text BEFORE its current fork,
and its assignment. It cannot read another sibling. Shared setup and Plans
must not calculate the unknown results assigned to siblings. A Summary must
accurately list the actual established outputs with unchanged variable meanings,
and expose them for later rounds. Numeric results that must be combined before
another fork belong in that Summary. Do not silently jump to the final answer.
Useful independent verification branches are allowed: explicitly assign a
substantive needed quantity and independently recompute it from pre-fork inputs.
Different strategies are useful, but the same method is also allowed when it
provides a real check. Compare results explicitly in Summary and resolve any
disagreement. Normally use at most one verification branch per round and keep
most branches doing distinct needed work. Repeating the same numerical result
is not itself an error; copying it or padding a fork with identities is.
Prefer no verification if it would be decorative. Every output must be consumed
downstream or in the
explicit comparison. Carefully recheck arithmetic in Summary and the final merge.
